What an open belt costs in the process

CCB sells on avoided process costs, not on the belt itself: product loss, cleaning, contaminated mechanics and downtime. Performance is confirmed per application with material tests.

Product loss
Material that falls along an open belt is product your customer paid for.
Cleaning
Spilled material has to be removed, around and under the conveyor.
Contaminated mechanics
Dust and dirt settle on drive rollers and bearings.
Downtime
Every cleaning or repair round can stop the line.

How it works

  1. An open belt

    On an open belt, material can fall off along the edges, and dust settles on rollers and bearings.

  2. The product stays in its own zone

    The housing encloses the product flow. Dry bulk is kept separate from the drive, the bearings and the return belt.

  3. Belt edges outside the housing

    The belt edges run outside the housing. That means less material lost along the belt.

  4. Labyrinth seals, lid seal and flap seal

    A labyrinth seal above and below the belt, plus a lid seal and a flap seal, keep material in.

  5. Fewer moving parts in the product flow

    The belt slides over a plate and returns through guides. Drives and bearings stay out of the product flow, so less dirt builds up on the drive rollers.

Where CCB fits

CCB was first developed for recycling and waste. It suits installation builders for dry bulk in several sectors. Suitability for each product is confirmed with material tests.

Your customers’ sectors

  • Recycling and waste

    Where CCB was first developed: household, medical and recyclable waste moved cleanly.

  • Feed, grain and fertiliser

    Dry, valuable bulk in internal transport, where spillage and dust cost money every day.

  • Chemicals, pharma and polymers

    Costly or sensitive products that should stay separate from the production area.

  • Cement, lime and minerals

    Installation builders for cement, lime, minerals and building materials.

Before a test: tell us about your material

With these details we can prepare a test on the rig. Please also tell us whether the material can be flammable or explosive.

  • Bulk density (kg/m³)
  • Particle size (min.-max., mm)
  • Moisture content (%)
  • Material temperature (°C)
  • Properties: sticky, abrasive, dusty, fragile, corrosive
  • Safety data sheet (SDS)
  • Desired throughput (t/h or m³/h)
  • Conveying length (m) and incline (°)
  • Sample quantity and packaging
